# Ravensmoor World Setting

A countryside of six independent villages, two woods and at least eight
multi-level dungeons forms the world around Ravensmoor. Magic is an ordinary
part of life. Elves inhabit the woods, household spirits share people's homes,
and dangerous creatures occupy caves, tunnels and abandoned places.

This is the setting foundation for adding locations, inhabitants and encounters.
It establishes no main plot, chosen hero or central villain. Ravensmoor, Greybridge and the
Undercrypt are established locations; the additional names, connections and
dungeon floor counts below are working choices.

The [recurring character cast](characters.md) develops the people and
relationships that can carry an episodic campaign across these places.

## Character of the world

The inhabited countryside feels familiar: gardens, mills, wells, workshops,
small markets and paths between neighbouring villages. Woods and underground
places introduce wonder, uncertainty and danger. The atmosphere can be eerie
without making every village miserable or every magical creature hostile.

There is no kingdom governing the region. Villages manage their own affairs
through councils, elders or whichever arrangements suit the community. They
cooperate over roads, bridges, trade and shared troubles. Disagreements tend to
concern everyday matters such as water, grazing, access and obligations.

People know nearby places better than distant ones. A traveller can hear
different names and explanations for the same landmark. The wider world can
remain undefined while the playable region grows.

## Villages and travel

The six villages share a modest building vocabulary: timber and stone houses,
plastered walls, tiled or thatched roofs, fences, wells and lanterns. Reuse the
same village assets. Surroundings, occupations, people and a few landmarks give
each village its identity; each does not need a separate architectural style.

| Village | Surroundings and everyday life | Local connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ravensmoor | A village with a small market, workshops and an old quarter. The existing courtyard belongs here. | The central meeting place for several local routes; the Undercrypt opens beneath it. |
| Brookstead | Gardens, a mill and small workshops along the upper brook. Practical household and craft magic is common. | A road follows the brook towards Ravensmoor. |
| Alderford | A settlement beside a ford at the edge of the Elderwood. Villagers gather woodland produce and trade with elves. | A western route from Ravensmoor crosses here before reaching the forest paths. |
| Greybridge | A village around the region's main river crossing. Carters, repairers and traders stop here. | A road links its river market with Ravensmoor and the downstream villages. |
| Reedbank | A fishing and reed-cutting village downstream, with wet meadows, boats and marsh paths. | Connected to Greybridge by river and a bank-side track. |
| Bracken Hollow | Farms and grazing land among low hills. People know the nearby caves and old burial grounds by local names. | A track branches from the Brookstead road into the hills. |

These connections establish neighbours rather than a final map. Travel should
offer a mixture of ordinary roads, river crossings and less certain woodland
paths. Some dungeons lie close to settlements; others require leaving the main
routes. A village can serve several nearby destinations, and a dungeon can have
more than one entrance.

Villages are places to rest, obtain supplies, exchange news and meet recurring
people. They should feel inhabited beyond whatever the player happens to need.
Neighbours have friendships, relatives, habits and disagreements of their own.

## Magic and religion

Magic is common enough that ordinary people use it and plan around it. A baker
may know a heat charm, a gardener may ward seedlings, and a ferryman may carry a
knotted cord that warns of dangerous water. Charms, potions, enchanted tools and
minor magical services belong in village trade.

Common magic still requires materials, maintenance or learned skill. A lamp
needs fuel or a renewed charm. A protection works within particular limits.
Healing and stronger spells require more expertise than lighting a hearth.
Magic makes useful things possible without making darkness, injury, distance
or hard work disappear. The deeper dungeons remain poorly lit and dangerous.

Knowledge is spread among craftspeople, herbalists, village practitioners,
elves and other beings. There is no need for a dominant magical academy or a
single authority explaining every supernatural occurrence.

Religion has a quieter role. Small shrines, burial customs, seasonal observances
and personal beliefs can exist without a powerful regional church. A village
might tend a roadside shrine while treating its resident water spirit as a
neighbour with particular habits. Familiar magical practices need not all be
acts of worship. A detailed pantheon is unnecessary at this stage.

## The two woods

### Elderwood

The Elderwood borders Alderford and contains elven homes, gathering places and
paths maintained over many generations. These can be modest dwellings and
clearings within the trees; an elaborate new architectural set is unnecessary.

Elves have their own livelihoods, families and disagreements. Some regularly
visit the villages, while others prefer visitors to keep to agreed paths.
Relationships with villagers differ between households and communities.

Household charms, plant lore and woodland craft pass between neighbours.
Entering deeper parts of the wood requires more knowledge of paths, boundaries
and local inhabitants than walking along its edge.

### Thornwood

The Thornwood lies beyond the hills near Bracken Hollow. It is less regularly
travelled, with thick undergrowth, damp hollows and paths that are easy to lose.
Elves know parts of it, but neither elves nor villagers control the whole wood.

Forest spirits, animals and more dangerous creatures inhabit different areas.
Occasional clearings, shelters and inhabited spots keep it from becoming one
continuous hostile zone. Its dangers come from particular places and beings.

## European folklore

Draw selectively from European folk traditions and adapt them into a coherent
local countryside. Introduce beings through their homes, habits and relationships
with people rather than presenting a catalogue of every tradition.

- **House spirits**, inspired by hobs and brownies, may help in kitchens,
  workshops and barns. Particular households have their own customs for them.
- **Water beings**, inspired by nixies and related traditions, inhabit pools,
  streams and millponds. Some bargain or exchange favours; others are dangerous.
- **Forest guardians**, inspired in part by leshy traditions, care about
  particular woods, boundaries or living things.
- **Will-o'-the-wisps** appear over wet ground and uncertain paths. Their
  behaviour need not have the same explanation everywhere.
- **Mine and earth spirits** belong around workings, buried passages and caves.
  Villagers may distinguish these from the kobold communities living underground.

Small customs make these relationships tangible: leaving a portion of a meal,
asking before cutting a particular tree, or avoiding a pool after dusk. Customs
vary locally. Not every superstition must be true, and supernatural beings need
not share a universal set of rules.

This is an original setting. Borrow broad folklore and fantasy ideas while
creating our own places, characters and descriptions; official D&D setting lore
is not part of the world.

## Dungeons

Plan for eight dungeon sites, each with two or more connected levels. A dungeon
level is an explorable map or floor; eight sites means substantially more than
eight maps. Floor counts are planning assumptions and can change with level
design.

Reuse stone corridors, cave surfaces, doors and props across sites. Layout,
lighting, inhabitants, objects and environmental conditions can distinguish them
without requiring eight architectural themes. Their history can be local:
unused workings, expanded cellars and old burial places do not all need to be
remnants of a single lost empire.

| Dungeon site | Access and character | Working levels | Suitable existing monsters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Undercrypt | Beneath Ravensmoor; the existing labyrinth can become the upper part of a deeper complex. | 3 | Snakes, rats, kobolds and skeletons, with stronger creatures deeper in. |
| Old Quarry Warrens | Near Greybridge; abandoned workings lead into natural passages. | 3 | Kobolds, goblins, orcs and an ogre. |
| Drowned Cellars | Near Reedbank; connected storage chambers descend towards flooded passages. | 3 | Rats, snakes, zombies and ghouls. |
| Barrow Hill | Outside Bracken Hollow; burial passages connect beneath several mounds. | 3 | Skeletons, zombies, ghouls and spiders. |
| Root Hollows | Beneath the Elderwood; roots and natural caves cross buried chambers. | 3 | Spiders, snakes and an owlbear in a suitable large den. |
| The Broken Kilns | Outside Brookstead; old workshops, stores and clay workings connect below ground. | 2 | Rats, goblins, kobolds and spiders. |
| Mosswell Caves | Beyond Alderford; a spring marks the entrance to a deeper cave system. | 3 | Snakes, spiders and a troll near deep water. |
| Thorn Vaults | Within the Thornwood; enclosed passages open into larger underground spaces. | 4 | Goblins, orcs, skeletons, an ogre and a troll in separate suitable areas. |

This gives a working total of 24 dungeon levels. They can be added gradually;
the setting does not require all of them to be implemented before play expands
beyond Ravensmoor.

## Creatures and inhabitants

Distribute the existing twelve monster types across the dungeons. A site should
have a recognizable population, with occasional overlap between neighbouring
habitats, rather than contain the entire bestiary. Its layout should accommodate
its inhabitants: large creatures need suitable chambers and routes, while rats
and spiders can occupy neglected corners and upper levels.

The Undercrypt already contains a broad selection of creatures. That does not
require future dungeons to contain every type. Redistribute ordinary encounters
as new sites become playable while retaining encounters needed by existing
adventures. These population suggestions do not change the current maps.

Creature identity does not determine every encounter. Goblins or kobolds may
have camps, neighbours and reasons to bargain. Animals may defend dens. Spirits
may be indifferent, helpful or hostile depending on their circumstances.
Combat encounters can coexist with conversations and peaceful inhabitants.

## Principles for expansion

Give each new place a reason to exist, neighbours, ordinary inhabitants and
something worth exploring. Keep its connection to the surrounding countryside
understandable. Add enough local history to explain what the player sees,
leaving room for later details.

Maintain the shared pixel-art village and dungeon vocabulary. Distinguish places
through their use and contents before commissioning new environmental art.
Keep common magic visible in everyday life, organized religion modest, and
village authority local. Develop stories within this world when needed without
making its geography depend on a single campaign plot.

# The pixel slice (`game-2/px.html`)

A reversible test of **natively generated pixel art** in the 2D compositing demo. The same courtyard
as `game-2/index.html`, but the view is drawn at a low internal resolution (**160x90** to start with) and
shown larger with hard edges, and some of its art is generated by the pixel-art LoRA, not by resampling a
painting. The default demo is untouched and keeps working.

## Configuration: one value

Resolution and canvas factor are set in one place, `PX` in `game-2/quarter-px.js`:

```js
export const PX = { width: 160, height: 90, factor: 8 };
```

`width` x `height` is the internal resolution (the view canvas and every slot image); `factor` is how many
times larger the painting is than that, i.e. the size of one art pixel in the painting. Everything else is
derived: the display scale (640 / width), the slot-size threshold, the painting canvas
(`width*factor` x `height*factor`, written to `game-2/px/art/plan.json`), the guides, the lattice period of
the slicer and the cell size. To rebuild the slice at **320x180 painted with factor 4** (a 1280x720 canvas,
for a LoRA trained on art enlarged 4x), change the two numbers to `320`, `180`, `4` and run `bake --px`,
`guides --px` and paint again; the art folder must be rebuilt, painted art from another setting does not
fit. (Checked: with 320/180/4 the bake, the guides, a slice round trip and the page all work.)

## Why it exists

Pixel art draws its outlines knowing the pixel size, so a finished picture cannot be turned into pixel
art by shrinking it. The only generator here that draws on a real pixel grid is the `pixel-art-xl` LoRA
for SDXL: it draws **one art pixel per cell of SDXL's 8x8 latent grid**. A first pilot showed that the
8 px lattice survives in image-to-image with the LoRA at 1.0 and a canny ControlNet at 0.9, and that
taking every 8th pixel of the result gives real pixel art with one-pixel outlines.

**The factor rule:** the canvas must be `factor` times the internal resolution, and `factor` is a property of
the generator: 8 for the stock `pixel-art-xl` LoRA (SDXL's latent cell), 4 for a LoRA trained on art enlarged
only 4x. With factor 8 a 160x90 view is painted on a 1280x720 canvas; 240x135 would need 1920x1080 (about a minute per image on 8 GB); 320x180 would need
2560x1440, which does not fit. The guide (the placeholder panel enlarged by whole pixels) must sit on the
same lattice.

## Run it

Serve the repository root (`python -m http.server 8000`) and open `/game-2/px.html`. The controls are
the same as in the default demo, which has two display keys (both pages also show them as buttons): **P**
switches how the view canvas is scaled up to the screen between **sampling** (nearest neighbour, crisp art
pixels, the default) and **smooth scaling** (bilinear); **R** cycles the internal resolution (the default demo
draws 640x360, 320x180 and 160x90 from the same painted slot images; the pixel slice has only its own
160x90, so R just says so). Both choices are remembered in `localStorage` (`view-scaling`, `view-res`) when the
browser allows it. The default demo is `/game-2/index.html`.

## What is in it

| | |
|---|---|
| `game-2/px.html` | entry point (a copy of `index.html` that loads the variant below) |
| `game-2/quarter-px.js` | the configuration `PX` and the same level with the matching low-resolution view |
| `game-2/px/assets/` | slot images at 160x90 scale: placeholders baked from experiment 1, plus the painted ones |
| `game-2/px/art/` | one-panel guides (`guides/`), `plan.json`, painted sheets (`painted/`) |
| `tools/comfy/paint-px.mjs` | `paint <sheet> --px`: SDXL + LoRA + ControlNet at 1280x720 |

Painted and placeholder art are told apart in the results section below. Everything not painted is the
experiment-1 placeholder baked at 160x90 (smoothly downscaled, hard-edged, no soft shadows), so it looks
blurrier than the painted parts.

## Pipeline

```
node tools/studio2d.mjs bake --px                 placeholders -> game-2/px/assets/
node tools/studio2d.mjs guides --px               one panel per sheet, enlarged by `factor` -> game-2/px/art/guides/
node tools/studio2d.mjs paint walls-house-a-1 --px [--denoise 0.8] [--seed N] [--slice]
node tools/studio2d.mjs slice walls-house-a-1 game-2/px/art/painted/walls-house-a-1.png --px
```

- **One panel per generation.** A sheet is one scene at the internal resolution (1280x720 painted at 160x90 x8). House-a takes 4
  generations, a prop 4-10.
- **Slicing reads one pixel per cell** (`factor` x `factor`) (the median of the cell, at the lattice phase found from the
  painting), never resamples. Slot masks are hard (a pixel is in or out), the magenta key is cut without a
  fringe, left-side slots are flipped by whole pixels. It prints how well the painting sits on the lattice:
  `share` is the fraction of colour change that falls on the lattice lines (1.00 is perfect, random 1/factor).
- **Painting settings** (`paint-px.mjs`): LoRA 1.0, canny ControlNet 0.9 (blur 2, thresholds 0.10/0.30),
  denoise 0.8, 30 steps, Euler/normal, cfg 7, 1280x720, plain VAE, no IP-Adapter (the concept-art
  reference greyed the colours), exact magenta key stamped back over the result. The floor starts from a
  blurred guide at denoise 0.9 and takes a depth ramp or the edges of a painted floor as its layout.

## Engine and tool changes (all inert by default)

- `engine2d/view.js`: an optional view setting `pixel: n`. Only when it is set, prop rectangles are whole
  pixels and slot masks grow by half a pixel instead of 0.75.
- `engine2d/compositor.js`: only for a pixel view, `imageSmoothingEnabled = false` and no soft shadow.
- `engine2d/game.js`: the displayed size is `W * (view.pixel ?? 1)`; with no `pixel` setting it is exactly
  what it was. It also owns the display keys: P (sampling / smooth, via `image-rendering` on the view canvas)
  and R (internal resolution: the view is composed in its own W x H coordinates and drawn into a smaller
  canvas through a transform).
- `tools/studio2d/plan.js`: `SHEET_PX`, and an optional `sheet` argument of `makePlan` and `sceneRect`.
- `tools/studio2d/studio.js`: `studio.html?px` selects the pixel level, the `game-2/px/` folders, hard
  alpha for placeholders and the pixel slicer. Without `?px` nothing changes.
- `tools/studio2d.mjs`, `tools/comfy/paint.mjs`: the `--px` flag (a two-line hook in `paint.mjs`).

The default demo was compared before and after these changes: its start view and five other views (the
view and HUD canvases, 12 canvases) are byte-identical when the changed engine files run on the assets as
they were before, and the 36-view sweep reports 0 missing images on both pages.

## How to revert

Delete `game-2/px/`, `game-2/px.html`, `game-2/quarter-px.js` and `tools/comfy/paint-px.mjs`. That removes
the slice. The engine and tool edits above do nothing without a `pixel` view setting or `--px`, so they
can stay; to remove them too, undo the listed edits (a copy of each original was kept).

## What was painted (first test, 160x90, factor 8)

Painted natively: the **house-a** wall type (4 panels, all 16 slots) and the **floor** (1 panel). Everything
else (other wall types, props, NPCs, backdrop) is the placeholder baked at 160x90. The stall was not painted.

| Generation | Seconds | Lattice (share x/y, random 0.12) | Flat 8x8 blocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| walls-house-a-1 | 27 | 0.44 / 0.36 | 0.88 |
| walls-house-a-2 | 27 | 0.47 / 0.39 | 0.88 |
| walls-house-a-3 | 38 | 0.50 / 0.44 | 0.89 |
| walls-house-a-4 | 27 | 0.54 / 0.43 | 0.89 |
| floor, 6 attempts (best: seed 4) | 26-27 | 0.33-0.38 / 0.35-0.46 | 0.95-0.99 |

All 10 generations had the 8 px lattice at phase 0 in all four quadrants, so none had to be rejected. The
walls kept their layout and the exact key. The floor used the edges of the painted Flux floor as ControlNet
(`mkfloorcontrol.py`-style: downscale to 160x90, Sobel, enlarge x8), because a depth ramp alone gives a flat
ground. Its wording matters a lot: "rose-mauve / warm brown" produced dirt and grass, "wall" or "brick" words
produce brick walls, "purple" produces cold blue-grey stone. The kept floor is the cold one with its hue shifted
by a plain colour grade (the grade keeps the lattice) and the horizon strip keyed out.

### Verdict

- **The native pixel look works.** Outlines are one art pixel wide, the 8x8 cells are flat, and the slices fit
  the engine with no seams from the slicing itself.
- **It does not match the rest yet.** The painted house-a is more saturated and yellower than the placeholder
  houses (cream plaster comes out yellow, roofs bright orange), so it reads as a patch next to the downscaled
  placeholders. The floor is lighter and bluer than the surroundings, its stones read as slabs and bricks, and
  it has pink grout specks.
- **The placeholders look blurry next to it.** They are smooth downscales, not pixel art, so a mixed scene looks
  worse than an all-painted one would. Painted art needs to cover whole scenes.
- Layout follows the guide loosely (some beams and windows move by a few art pixels).

### Cost of a full roll-out (factor 8, 160x90)

Panels per art: 4 for each of the 6 wall types (24), 4 to 10 for each of the 9 props (70 in all), plus the
floor and the backdrop: about 95 generations at 27-38 s, roughly an hour of GPU time for one pass, several
passes with re-rolls. A strict palette across all of them (which the colours above need) is not solved by the
LoRA alone.
